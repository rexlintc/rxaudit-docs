What it checks

The checks look at your prescriptions, fills, reversals and sign-offs, and cite College of Pharmacists of BC bylaws (PODSA), the PharmaNet conformance standards and BC PharmaCare policy.

Claims to PharmaNet

Fills that were claimed and should have been reversed
  • 05

    Filled, never picked up, never reversed

    Fills over 30 days old with no signature or delivery record that are still claimed to PharmaNet.

    PharmaNet Conformance Standards PNet22.2 · PODSA Bylaws s.35(4)

    high
  • 04

    Late reversal, or reversal with no reason

    Unclaimed fills reversed more than 30 days late, or reversed with no reason recorded.

    PharmaNet Conformance Standards PNet22.2 · PODSA Bylaws s.35(4)

    high

Dispensing within the prescription

More than was authorized, or after it expired
  • 01

    Dispensed more than authorized

    The amount handed out exceeds what the prescriber authorized, by quantity or by number of fills.

    PODSA Bylaws s.19(7)

    high
  • 03

    Filled after the prescription expired

    Fills dispensed after the prescription's legal validity period had run out.

    PODSA Bylaws s.23.1(3)

    high

Records and sign-off

What has to be on file
  • 08

    Controlled prescription with no scanned form

    Fills of duplicate-form drugs with no scanned prescription image on file.

    PODSA Bylaws s.19(6), s.23(4)

    high
  • 06

    Missing stop date

    Opioid agonist treatment and daily-dispensed prescriptions left with no stop date.

    Community Pharmacy Practice Standards

    medium
  • 09

    Workflow sign-off left blank

    Fills where a required sign-off in the workflow was never completed.

    Community Pharmacy Practice Standards

    medium
  • 10

    Fills whose prescription record is gone

    Dispenses whose prescription record was deleted while still inside the retention period.

    PODSA Bylaws s.23(1), s.23(2)

    medium

For review

A list to look over, not a list of problems
  • 12

    Frequency of dispensing authorization status

    For each blister-pack and daily-dispense patient, whether the authorization was sent. This check is new and has only run at one store, so confirm a row against how your staff actually send the form before acting on it.

    BC PharmaCare Frequency of Dispensing policy · PODSA Bylaws s.23(1)

    info

Running it

On the computer at your store that runs WinRx. It takes a few seconds.

  1. Unzip the package

    Unzip rxaudit.zip anywhere on the WinRx computer, for example the desktop.

  2. Open rxaudit.exe

    The first time, Windows may show a blue "Windows protected your PC" box, because it isn't signed yet. Click More info, then Run anyway.

  3. Check the connection

    It finds your WinRx database and fills in the connection boxes itself. You normally don't touch them.

  4. Pick a date range

    It starts at the last 90 days. Widen it to cover the period you care about, for example the months an auditor has asked about.

  5. Click Run audit

    The report opens in your web browser and a copy is saved in a reports folder next to the program.

Reading the report

The top of the report is a summary of every check. Below it, each check has its own table of findings.

Shortened. The real summary lists all nine checks with the rule each one cites.
  • Start with high. Look at high-severity findings first. Medium findings are work lists: records to complete, not emergencies.
  • Every finding names the prescription. Rx number, drug, dates and quantities, so you can open it in WinRx and see for yourself.
  • Zero can mean "doesn't apply". Checks 05 and 08 depend on your store recording pickups and scanning prescriptions electronically. If it mostly does that on paper, these checks stay quiet rather than flagging every fill, so a zero there is not proof that everything is clean.
  • The version is printed at the bottom. It's also in the window title. Quote it when you report a problem.

Your data stays at your store

  • Read-only. It only reads your WinRx database. It never changes, deletes or writes anything.
  • Nothing is sent anywhere. It runs entirely on your computer and sends nothing.
  • The report contains patient information. Keep it on that computer and please don't email it, including to me.

If something goes wrong

  • It can't connect. The connection boxes at the top of the window can be edited. If they're empty or the login fails, email me. It's usually the server name or the password for that store, and it takes a minute to fix over AnyDesk.
  • A finding looks wrong. Note the check number and tell me what's wrong with it, and we'll look at it together on your screen.

What I'd like to hear back

  1. Which findings were real, and which were false alarms? The check number is enough.

  2. If you've been audited recently, did rxaudit flag the same prescriptions the auditor asked about? What did they ask for that it missed?

  3. Would you have run this before that audit, and how often would you run it now?
